\subsection*{Why This Manuscript Fits \textit{JMIS}}
Enterprise Security Operations Centers (SOCs) increasingly deploy generative AI copilots and machine-learning triage pipelines as organizational decision-support systems. While prior Information Systems (IS) delegation and explainable AI (XAI) research assumes that linguistically coherent explanations improve human sensemaking and metaknowledge, SOC telemetry contains attacker-controllable string fields (\texttt{CommandLine}, \texttt{ProcessCommandLine}, and alert payload strings) that expose AI copilots and text-inclusive classifiers to \textbf{Indirect Telemetry Prompt Injection (ITPI)}. This manuscript addresses the core themes of \textit{JMIS} across four tightly integrated layers:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Behavioral and Decision-Theoretic IS Theory (DCEDM):} Building on \textit{JMIS} scholarship on human-AI collaboration and delegation (Revilla et al., 2023; Tutun et al., 2024; Ansari and Ghasemaghaei, 2026) and neuro-cognitive warning habituation (Vance et al., 2018; Jensen et al., 2022), we formulate the \textbf{Dual-Cue Epistemic Defeasibility Model (DCEDM)}. Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 formally derive how narrative fluency ($\Delta F = 0.70$) shifts posterior compromise log-odds downward, compresses optimal primary telemetry verification ($V^*$), and inflates expected Compromise Miss Rates (CMR), while characterizing exact cost-ratio dominance boundaries ($\kappa^*(\lambda_F)$) and an interior optimal gating threshold ($\tau^*_{\text{LLR}}$) under unified endogenous warning habituation.
  \item \textbf{Computational Cybersecurity Design Science Artifact (PITSA + Two-Stage DECS, DP1 to DP3):} Advancing the computational security design science stream in \textit{JMIS} (Ebrahimi et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2020; Li and Chai, 2022; Ampel et al., 2024), we design the \textbf{Prompt-Isolated Telemetry Discrepancy Scorer (PITSA)} and \textbf{Two-Stage Defeasible Epistemic Cueing System (DECS)} around three generalizable socio-technical design principles (\textbf{DP1 to DP3}). Proposition 1 proves that enforcing an architectural projection $\Pi_{\text{struct}}$ ($\nabla_{\mathcal{A}_{\text{text}}} L(e) \equiv 0$) guarantees $100\%$ invariance to text-channel prompt injection while providing white-box additive per-pillar forensic attribution ($k^*(e)$) in $2.12$ to $4.31\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ per event on a single CPU core.
  \item \textbf{Multi-Corpus Zero-Leakage Empirical Evaluation ($N = 2,238,434$ Real Telemetry Records):} Across four public cybersecurity corpora (\texttt{OTRF Security-Datasets}, $N=43,723$; \texttt{SALAD-SOC}, $N=416,781$; \texttt{LMD-2023}, $N=1,777,930$; and \texttt{CISA KEV}, $N=1,741$ CVEs; $671,531$ held-out test records), we demonstrate that five unpartitioned classifiers spanning lexical, dense semantic neural ($\text{SVD-48}+\text{MLP}(64,32)$), and contextual subword language model architectures suffer statistically significant recall collapse under both graded appended ITPI ($m \in \{1,2,4,8\}$) and full-override ITPI ($85.81\% \to 25.16\%$ for Logistic Regression and $65.81\% \to 0.00\%$ for Dense Neural MLP on OTRF; $77.46\%\text{ to }84.93\%$ clean recall dropping to $2.92\%\text{ to }36.05\%$ on SALAD-SOC; McNemar's $\chi^2 \ge 86.01$ on OTRF and $\chi^2 \ge 8,361.46$ on SALAD-SOC, all $p < 0.0001$). In contrast, Partitioned PITSA suffers $0.00\text{ pp}$ degradation ($\text{AUROC} = 0.9807$ on OTRF, $0.9380$ on SALAD-SOC, and $0.9963$ on LMD-2023).
  \item \textbf{Trace-Driven Governance Sensitivity and Explicit Scope Boundaries:} Trace-driven evaluation over held-out LLR streams establishes that Selective DECS ($\Pi_4$) reduces warning banner volume by $95.92\%$ (OTRF) and $79.87\%$ (SALAD-SOC), cuts expected operational loss by $30.16\%$ to $60.77\%$ on enterprise SIEM alerts ($\kappa^* = 1.68$), and achieves an interior U-shaped habituation optimum at $\tau^*_{\text{LLR}} = 1.0$. We also transparently quantify the exact adaptive Living-off-the-Land (LOLBin) boundary where 5-pillar mimicry ($\rho_{\text{mask}} = 1.0$) defeats single-event structural scoring.
\end{enumerate}
